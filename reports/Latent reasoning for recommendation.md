# Latents get read only when they know something new

The 2024–2026 literature points to one conclusion: **a latent reasoning vector carries useful, non-redundant information only when something outside the prompt puts that information there.** That source can be a teacher with world knowledge, a retrieval or graph signal the backbone cannot compute, or a training setup that blocks every other route to the answer. Extra latent steps, diversity regularisers and RL noise on the latent are not enough. **Not one of roughly 30 recommendation papers we reviewed (latent LLM, latent SASRec-style, or textual chain-of-thought) tests whether predictions depend on the latent's content** by swapping, zeroing or perturbing it at inference. The general-reasoning papers that do run these tests find that **bypass is the default**: Coconut tokens swapped across MMLU examples move accuracy from 60.9 to 61.0 ([Do latent tokens think?](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.21711)). Our R3 experiments reproduce this in recommendation: swapping r between users moves the output 0.006× as much as dropping one history item. The pattern is visible across the field but no paper names it. Latent gains over no-reasoning baselines are small (about +2% to +17%), are often confounded with RL or teacher supervision, and peak at 1–4 steps. Methods that add information from outside the prompt report the largest gains. For R3 on Amazon Musical Instruments, our miss analysis shows that interaction-derived signals (SASRec, graphs, SVD, category classifiers) are redundant with what R3 already gets right. The one candidate this literature supports and R3 has not tried is **teacher world knowledge about complementary products and category transitions**, distilled into the latent and checked with content-dependence tests.

## Twenty-odd recommenders, no swap test

Three families of recommenders produce latent reasoning, and textual chain-of-thought recommenders serve as the contrast class. The **LLM title line** follows LatentR3's protocol: Qwen2.5-1.5B, titles, Amazon Toys/CDs/Games/Instruments, history length 10. LatentR3, FLR, IntuRec, HiLaR and LaRec use it. The **semantic-ID (SID) line** uses Qwen3 with 4×256 SIDs: LASAR, PauseRec and EvoReason, plus the T5-based S²GR and IBA. The **ID-based sequential line** extends SASRec-style encoders with fed-back hidden states: ReaRec, LARES, PLR, ManCAR, DiffuReason and RecRec. The tables below record what each paper does. The "dependence test" column is where the field is uniformly weakest.

Two features of this landscape matter for R3. First, **the gains ride on presence and supervision, not on demonstrated content.** LatentR3's own Toys ablation gets most of its gain from SFT with the latent present (0.0701→0.0804); RL adds +0.0017 ([LatentR3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19092)). PauseRec beats a strong explicit-CoT system with **one shared pause embedding**, so the only per-user content comes from attention over the prompt ([PauseRec](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14142)). LASAR's latent loop with no teacher alignment adds **+0.4%** ([LASAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10207)). Our R3 experiments show the same thing directly: simultaneous latent-only + normal loss on CDs yields presence dependence (zeroing r costs −0.0247 HR@10) with no content dependence (swap and mean-replacement are null). That is the pause-token signature. Coconut's own ablation shows it in general reasoning: pause-as-thought nearly matches Coconut on ProntoQA and ProsQA (100.0 vs 99.8, 96.6 vs 97.0) ([Coconut](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06769)). Second, **cross-paper noise is as large as the effects.** LatentR3's Instruments H@5 is 0.1029 in FLR and 0.1032 in HiLaR. R²ec's Instruments has 15,656 users and SCoTER's has 24,772 ([R²ec](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16994), [SCoTER](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19514v6)). Only LaRec, HiLaR and LASAR report any significance testing.

## Bypass is the default; reliance needs new information or a blocked shortcut

The papers that actually intervene on latents all come from general reasoning, and they agree. Cui et al. add σ=100 Gaussian noise to latents and find that **Coconut, CODI and SIM-CoT are almost unaffected** on ProsQA. CODI with zeroed latents still scores about 30% on GSM8K. Explicit CoT under the same corruption collapses from 61.7 to 0.03 ([Cui et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.22441)). The same paper names the trade-off R3 faces: **stronger supervision reduces shortcuts but also reduces latent diversity**, and a curriculum fix brings Coconut's ProsQA-under-noise accuracy down to 4.4%, meaning the model now depends on its latents. Viveiros et al. find in four latent vision-language models that some replacement (noise, zeros, pause tokens) always stays within 2 points of the true latents. Generated latents retrieve their own target only 0.3–3.3% of the time, and their mutual cosine similarity is 0.82–0.98. Reliance appears only when **the latent becomes the sole path**: masking the relevant image region opens a 75% vs 59% gap, and a synthetic task where latents carry unique information gives 0.86 vs 0.25 (chance) ([Viveiros et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.18445)). CVRR makes this a design principle. It deletes image states before answering and trains with answer cross-entropy alone, so swapping states across examples drops V\* accuracy from **81.2 to 14.0** ([CVRR](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.06746)).

Our R3 findings sit exactly on this map. Injecting the target's category into the slot is read heavily (read ratio 13.6, HR@10 0.59), so **the channel works**. The learned latent is ignored because everything it could encode is already in the prompt's title evidence. The literature's anti-collapse tools are the wrong remedy here. ReaRec's KL-apart loss, FLR's orthogonality and RecRec's diversity penalty all reduce *homogeneity between steps* (ReaRec's step similarity ≈1.00 without them) ([ReaRec](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.22675), [FLR](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.26760v1), [RecRec](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.12945v1)). None of them supplies information the decoder lacks. Diverse but redundant latents are still bypassable.

The depth results lead to the same conclusion. Optimal depth is **N=1** in LatentR3 and IntuRec, about 2 in ReaRec and PLR, 3–4 in LARES and DiffuReason, and LASAR's RL drives mean depth from 4.20 to 2.47 ([IntuRec](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27684), [PLR](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03153), [LARES](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16865)). ReaRec's similarity heatmaps and ManCAR's converging adjacent-step KL indicate that **steps converge to a fixed point of the shared block** ([ManCAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20093)). Iterating a function of the prompt cannot produce information the prompt lacks; at best it reorganises what is there. RecRec is the only method that extrapolates past its training depth (best N of 12–16 on three datasets), and it decouples the reasoning state from the prediction state, which supports the view that architecture separation, not depth, is what matters.

## External knowledge, not deliberation, explains the larger gains

When gains are sorted by information source, a clear ordering appears. Pure-computation methods (ReaRec, LARES, PLR, DiffuReason, PauseRec, LatentR3, FLR) report **roughly 2–8% over strong baselines**, with negative cells in ReaRec and PLR. On Instruments the margins are thinnest: DiffuReason gains +3.73% R@5 there against +19.64% on Beauty, and FLR's H@5 gain over LatentR3 is +0.29% ([DiffuReason](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09744)). Methods that inject knowledge from outside the interaction data report the larger effects. **SCoTER distils DeepSeek-R1 chains into step embeddings and lifts HSTU on Beauty from 0.0930 to 0.1187 R@10, with tail items gaining +30.75% vs +21.42% for head items** and Tencent Ads GMV rising 2.14% online. Removing the student distillation drops it back to 0.1008 ([SCoTER](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19514v6)). KAR's world-knowledge augmentation and RecGPT's intent→tag→item pipeline report online gains, including +6.96% click category diversity for RecGPT ([KAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.10933), [RecGPT](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22879)).

The text-reasoning contrast class strengthens this reading. Every RL-based CoT recommender introduces reasoning and a metric-aligned RL objective together, so reasoning's own effect is never isolated. The two clean controls both cut against verbal deliberation. **Rec-R1's reasoning format is worse than no-reasoning JSON in 3 of 4 domains** ([Rec-R1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.24289)). **LangPTune's no-bottleneck contrastive model beats its RL-tuned text profiles** (0.0543 vs 0.0494) ([LangPTune](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.18870v2)). Several pipelines leak the target into training rationales: ThinkRec includes the label in the teacher prompt and emits "Yes/No" before reasoning, Reason4Rec gives the true rating as a retry hint, and OneRec-Think and RDRec condition on the target ([ThinkRec](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15091), [Reason4Rec](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02061)). In those systems the rationale acts as a privileged-information channel during training. Notably, privileged hindsight alone buys little in the latent setting: LaRec's target-oriented CoT beats non-target CoT on Instruments by only **0.1238 vs 0.1225**, while removing teacher step alignment costs much more (0.1142) ([LaRec](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.24617v1)). What transfers is the teacher's general knowledge, not the answer.

Collaborative signals behave differently on Instruments. ManCAR's Swing-graph context gives large CDs gains but **loses to ReaRec-PRL on Music** ([ManCAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.20093)). IntuRec found SASRec candidates *worse* than the LLM's own beam candidates (0.0900 vs 0.0986 R@5 on CDs) ([IntuRec](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.27684)). HiLaR's frozen-SASRec reward accompanies the best reported Instruments H@10 (0.1320), but no ablation shows that the CF reward is responsible ([HiLaR](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27760)). These outside results match our R3 experiments. A SASRec vector in the slot is read (ratio 1.10) but *substitutes* for title evidence (HR@10 0.1196 vs 0.1226). SASRec and R3 hit the same examples (81% overlap). Graph, PureSVD, SASRec and a trained next-category classifier all succeed on R3's hits and fail on its transition misses (next-leaf recall@1 ≈ 0.05 vs 0.03 popularity; item recall@10 ≤ 2.8%). On this dataset, **interaction-derived signals are the same information in a different form.**

The segment evidence completes the picture. Where the literature reports segments, latent and text reasoning gains concentrate on **sparse users and long-tail items** (ReaRec item group IG-1 +12–19%, DiffuReason sparse users +17.13%, SCoTER tail +30.75%) ([ReaRec](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.22675), [DiffuReason](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.09744)). **No paper measures performance on category transitions.** R3's misses are 95% transitions into mostly long-tail items, and R3's hits are continuations: same leaf category gives HR 0.61 vs 0.06, brand in history 0.42 vs 0.035. The literature's best-supported mechanism for tail gains (teacher knowledge about item semantics) is therefore aimed at exactly R3's failure set, though no published result confirms it works there.

## Selection is unsolved even when the right latent exists

A second, separate gap involves choosing among latents. ReaRec reports that an oracle choosing the best reasoning step per user raises the ceiling by **30–50%**, and PLR's oracle ceilings are similar, but neither model can make that choice itself ([ReaRec](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.22675), [PLR](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03153)). Our collaborative oracle has the same shape: best-of-paths across other samples' latents reaches HR@10 0.2275 vs 0.1450 for R3, and no retriever selects well. LASAR's depth policy head and ManCAR's KL halting choose *how much* to compute, not *which content* to use. Without new information, a selector faces the same problem as the decoder: from the prompt alone it cannot tell which path leads to a transition target. In our view this gap is downstream of the information gap, not separate from it.

## Prioritized recommendations for R3

The recommendations below follow from the literature plus our R3 experiments. They are ordered by expected information per GPU-hour. Each one keeps R3's existing content-dependence instruments (read ratio, swap, mean, zero) and adds a **continuation vs transition split**, since aggregate HR@10 hides where a non-redundant signal would act.

| Priority | Experiment | Why (evidence) | Pass criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| **P1** | **Gate test for teacher knowledge before any training.** A large LLM (e.g. Qwen-72B or a reasoning model) sees the history only, never the target, and writes "likely next needs / complementary products / next categories". Score its outputs on R3's transition misses with the same metrics used for graph, SVD, SASRec and the classifier | Every interaction-based signal failed this test; teacher world knowledge is the literature's main source of tail gains (SCoTER, KAR, RecGPT) and is untested on transitions anywhere | Next-leaf recall@1 clearly above 0.05 and item recall@10 clearly above 2.8% on transition misses, while not simply duplicating hits |
| **P2** | **Oracle-style injection of the teacher signal into the `<\|Thought\|>` slot** (encode the teacher text, project into the slot, the way the SASRec vector was injected) | The category-oracle result (ratio 13.6) proves the channel reads informative content; this measures whether a *realistic* complementary signal is read *and* helps, rather than substituting like SASRec | Read ratio well above the 0.006 baseline, HR@10 above 0.1226, gains concentrated in the transition segment, hit overlap with SASRec/R3 falling below 81% |
| **P3** | **Distil the teacher into r so inference needs no teacher.** Align r to the teacher-text hidden states with LASAR-style symmetric KL on softmaxed states or CODI-style single-anchor L1; avoid plain MSE; then LR-GRPO | LASAR: KL alignment +2.9% vs +0.4% unaligned, MSE −11.6% ([LASAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10207)); CODI: removing alignment drops GSM8K 43.7→24.5 ([CODI](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.21074)); SCoTER shows the distilled form keeps the gain | Swap/mean tests on r become non-null (content dependence, not just presence), with HR gains in the transition segment |
| **P4** | **Give r a job the prompt cannot do: factorise "where next" from "which title".** Supervise r to predict the teacher's next-need/category representation, and let the decoder handle title selection | RecGPT's intent→tag→item, S²GR/IBA per-level latents and RecRec's decoupled reasoning state all separate roles; the trained classifier failed only because its inputs lacked new information, so pair this with P1–P3 | Next-category accuracy decoded from r exceeds the 0.05 classifier on transitions |
| **P5** | **Learned selector over the collaborative paths, conditioned on teacher features.** Train a verifier, using the target-perplexity reward, to rank candidate latents from other samples | The 0.2275 vs 0.1450 oracle gap is large, but ReaRec/PLR show selection from prompt-only features fails; teacher features are the only new input available to a selector | Recovers a meaningful fraction of the oracle gap |
| **P6** | **Reporting hygiene.** At least 3 seeds; swap/zero/noise results next to every HR number; Viveiros-style self-retrieval and inter-latent cosine as collapse metrics | Cross-paper variance on Instruments matches the claimed gains; no recommendation paper reports a latent intervention, so R3's swap result is itself a first | Every claimed gain survives seed variance and a content-dependence test |

The experiments to **deprioritise** follow directly from the evidence. More latent steps peak at N=1–4 across eight papers and converge to fixed points. Further CF, graph or SASRec signals, including HiLaR-style SASRec rewards, are redundant with R3's hits on this dataset by our own measurements. Diversity regularisers address homogeneity, not redundancy. CVRR-style bypass blocking (hiding history from the decoder) would force content dependence, but it would make r carry the *same* history information, so it is useful only as a diagnostic: the category oracle already shows the channel works. Two risks remain. If P1 fails, the transition misses may be largely unpredictable from a 10-item history, and the realistic ceiling sits much closer to 0.12 than to 0.59. Second, a teacher that sees the target (LaRec, ThinkRec style) will look good in training but adds little at test time, as LaRec's 0.1238 vs 0.1225 shows. Keep the P1 teacher history-only.

## Conclusion

The field has been measuring whether a latent *slot* helps, not whether the latent's *content* is used. The difference matters because a slot helps through presence: extra computation, a learned bias, an attention sink. Content only helps when it carries information the decoder cannot obtain another way. R3's swap result, combined with the general-reasoning intervention studies, makes this the main open problem in latent recommendation, and R3's continuation-vs-transition analysis identifies where non-redundant information has to come from. On Amazon Musical Instruments, the information R3 lacks is knowledge about what people buy *next to* what they own, and interaction logs contain too little of it for the long tail. The next decisive result for R3 is not a better latent architecture. It is a yes-or-no answer from P1 on whether any available knowledge source predicts transitions.

A positive P1 result would give R3 a contribution the literature lacks: a latent recommender whose gain is shown to come from content (swap-sensitive), localised to category transitions, and traced to a named knowledge source. A negative result would also be publishable, since it would establish an empirical ceiling for history-only next-item prediction on this benchmark and explain why the latent-reasoning recommenders reviewed here mostly report single-digit percentage gains.
